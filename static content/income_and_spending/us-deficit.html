US Deficit

Explore the gap between federal spending and net receipts/income across fiscal years. A deficit occurs when spending exceeds receipts; a surplus occurs when receipts exceed spending. This project uses Monthly Treasury Statement (MTS) Tables 4, 5 and 6 for those measures.

Notes and Observations

Deficit is calculated as Net Outlays - Net Receipts. This means that in reports deficit will be reported as a "positive" dollar value.

Reading the Data

  • Compare complete, equivalent periods. Do not compare a partial current fiscal year against a full prior fiscal year.
  • Check the dashboard's coverage dates and active filters before drawing conclusions.
  • Displayed values are rounded to the nearest billion for clarity. Don't be fooled by "small looking numbers". Also, this can introduce some marginal rounding errors.

A deficit measures the gap over a reporting period; it is not the total outstanding federal debt. Differences from the official Table 1 summary are explained in the reconciliation methodology.

The total outstanding federal debt is on the report (Total Deficit). This view on the deficit dashboard only rolls up to Fiscal Year. As a result, the values for total deficit will not change when using Month filters.

Interest Payments on National Debt

The two classifications that capture interest payments made on our national debt are "Public Issues (Accrual Basis)" and "Special Issues (Cash Basis)". "Public Issues" represent the interest the government owes to real-world investors like everyday citizens, banks, and foreign countries who bought regular Treasury bonds; this interest is counted day by day as it adds up. "Special Issues" represent internal interest the government owes to itself, specifically when the U.S. Treasury borrows money from government programs like Social Security and Medicare; this interest is only counted on a cash basis when the money is officially moved into those programs' accounts. Together, these two categories make up the total annual interest bill on the national debt.

Data Sources and Processing

This dashboard brings together MTS Table 4: Receipts of the U.S. Government and MTS Table 5: Outlays of the U.S. Government, published by the U.S. Department of the Treasury. Table 4 supplies net receipts after refunds; Table 5 supplies net outlays after applicable receipt offsets. Compare these measures for matching reporting periods to examine the deficit or surplus.

In addition, to report on "Total Outstanding Federal Debt", we use MTS Table 6: Means of financing. We filter Table 6 to detail records with data_type_cd = D and include only the classifications “Debt Held by Public” and “Intragovernmental Holdings”. These debt balances provide context alongside the deficit, which measures the gap between spending and receipts over a selected period.
