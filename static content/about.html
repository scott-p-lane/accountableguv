Purpose and perspective

About Accountable Government

Accountable Government is an independent personal project exploring how public data can help people understand government finances, assess decisions, and participate in informed, constructive discussion.

Connecting data to decisions

In my work as a technology manager, I brought together spending, business operations, software delivery, sales, and usage data to understand the results of business decisions. Connecting those sources made it possible to examine investment returns, service costs, and customer profitability, and to give leaders a clearer basis for action.

This project began with a question: could that same approach help make government performance easier to understand? Public agencies publish substantial amounts of information, but availability alone does not make that information accessible. Records spread across systems can be difficult to interpret, compare, or connect to the decisions behind them.

Accountable Government is an opportunity to investigate those connections firsthand while developing deeper knowledge of public datasets and the engineering needed to make them useful.

Making public information useful

The long-term goal is to connect government receipts and spending with legislation, policy, programs, and operations, then present the evidence in clear, accessible terms. That means asking what was invested, what outcomes were intended, who was affected, and what the available data can tell us about the results.

The aim is to evaluate evidence without favoring a political party or predetermined conclusion. Government outcomes can involve competing priorities and cannot always be reduced to a financial return. Meaningful assessment requires explicit success criteria, appropriate context, and a clear account of uncertainty. A relationship between two datasets is a starting point for investigation; it does not by itself establish cause and effect.

The current site starts with the U.S. Treasury's Monthly Treasury Statement and a dashboard of federal net receipts. Connecting financial data to policy outcomes remains a development goal. The government data sources page explains the implemented architecture, source data, transformations, and limitations.

Learning from existing work

USAFacts is an inspiration for this project. Its approach to making government data understandable reflects the value I hope to contribute. This project is also a personal learning effort: an opportunity to work directly with the sources, understand their limitations, and build the systems that support useful analysis.

Accountable Government is a separate project and is not affiliated with USAFacts. You can find a link to USAFacts in our recommended resources.

Engineering and the use of AI

I am a certified AWS Solutions Architect, and this project gives me hands-on experience with software and cloud engineering beyond the opportunities available in my management work. It uses cloud services where practical, including AWS for data processing and hosting, and Tableau for visualization.

I use AI tools, including Codex, to help generate and validate HTML, Python scripts, and infrastructure configuration. I define the requirements and approach, review and refine the output, and remain responsible for the implementation. These tools help one person work through the detail of complex systems while continuing to learn how those systems operate.

Transparency applies to the engineering as well as the analysis. Readers should be able to understand where the data came from, how it was processed, and which conclusions are supported by it.

A longer-term vision for civic discussion

Over time, I hope to create space for balanced, evidence-based discussion about government decisions and the work of elected officials. Shared facts can provide a stronger foundation for evaluating performance than campaign messaging alone.

Discussion features are an aspiration, not a current capability. Building them would require a thoughtful approach to moderation and participation so that conversation remains useful and respectful.

The broader ambition is to bring public officials and the people they represent into open discussion of evidence, outcomes, and possible improvements. Accountability becomes more practical when people can understand the information, question the conclusions, and work together across party affiliations.
